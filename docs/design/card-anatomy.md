Every card in sc-tcg is built from the same set of parts. Knowing what each part does makes both playing and deck-building much easier — and gives the art process a stable frame to work with (card illustrations are a key part of the project; see the [Blog](../../posts/) for design reflections).

> *Draft — layout names below will be locked in once the first card mockups exist.*

## The parts of a card

| Part | What it holds | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Name** | The unit or ability's name | Faction flavor lives here (e.g. StarCraft unit names) |
| **Artwork** | The card illustration | Central to the project's identity — see [History & Influences](../overview/history.html) for art direction context |
| **Cost** | Resources required to play the card | Interacts with the turn structure in [Core Rules](../rules/core-rules.html#turn-structure) |
| **Type line** | Which of the [card types](../rules/card-types.html) this is (Unit / Ability / Support — draft names) | Determines what rules apply to it |
| **Text box** | Written abilities and effects | The MTG-inspired part; keywords will be defined in a dedicated article later |
| **Flavor text** *(planned)* | In-universe quote, no game effect | Ties cards back to the StarCraft setting |

## Why this anatomy

The split follows directly from [Design Principles](design-principles.html): a fast-readable top (name + art + cost) for table speed, and a structured text box that can carry real rules depth without ambiguity.
